Personal or organization

Not every developer has to run a closed test. Which path you are on depends on the type of account and when it was created.

The distinction that matters

The 12-tester, 14-day rule is written for newly created personal developer accounts. An organization account follows a different verification path, generally centred on confirming the business behind the account rather than on demonstrating a test audience.

Working out which one you have

You do not have to guess, and you should not take a blog post's word for it, including this one. Play Console displays the requirement that applies to your account. Look for the production access step and read what it asks for:

  • If it points you at running a closed test with a tester count and a duration, you are on the personal-account path.
  • If it asks about your organization and its verification status, you are on the organizational path.

The console is the authority

Policy changes. What matters is the wording attached to your own account on the day you read it, because that is what your application will be judged against.

Common misunderstandings

  • Switching type later. Converting or moving an account is not a way to sidestep an unmet requirement retroactively.
  • Assuming the date is the whole test. The creation date matters, but so does the account type. Both need to line up.
  • Assuming a paid account is exempt. Paying the registration fee is separate from the testing requirement.

If you are on the personal path

Then the work is straightforward, even if it is tedious: a closed track, twelve opted-in testers, fourteen continuous days, then an application. The walkthrough covers the sequence, and adding testers covers the mechanics that stall most attempts.

If you are on the organizational path

Your test does not have to satisfy the 12-tester rule, but a closed test is still worth running before a public release. A fortnight of real usage before launch is useful regardless of which rule applies to your account, and it is the cheapest way to find the crashes that only appear on hardware you do not own.
