Your 14 days did not count

You ran a test, you waited, and the option to apply is still locked. In nearly every case the cause is the same word in Google's sentence: continuously.

The rule asks for testers who have been opted in for at least the last 14 days continuously. That word does the damage, because it means the qualifying period is measured backwards from today, not from the day you started.

What continuity actually means

If your opted-in count sat at twelve for five days, dropped to ten for two days, then returned to twelve for nine more, you do not have fourteen continuous days. You have the most recent stretch, which at that point is nine days. The clock did not pause. It restarted.

Day twelve of a broken window is genuinely day twelve of a new one, no matter how much work went into the first attempt.

The four causes, in order of frequency

  • Drop-outs. Two or three testers quietly uninstall on day eight. Nobody notices until the count is read.
  • Tester swaps. Replacing a tester is fine, as long as the count never dips below twelve while you do it.
  • The test was stopped. Pausing or ending a track interrupts the window.
  • The count never reached twelve in the first place. Eleven opted-in testers for a month is still eleven.

The habit that prevents all four

Read the opted-in count once a day, and treat any dip as urgent rather than as something to look at tomorrow. A dip you fix within a day costs you a day. A dip you find on day fourteen costs you fourteen.

How to restart properly

  1. Stop adding people at random and audit the current list: who is genuinely opted in right now?
  2. Get the count comfortably above twelve, not exactly twelve.
  3. Note the date it holds. That is day one of the new window, and it is the date you will quote in your application.
  4. Check daily for the full fourteen days, and replace anyone who goes inactive immediately.
  5. Apply only once fourteen continuous days are genuinely behind you.

Why spare testers are not padding

A buffer of two or three people is the cheapest insurance in this process. They do nothing when everything goes well, and they absorb a drop-out without the count dipping at all when it does not. If you are recruiting by hand, recruiting fifteen instead of twelve is the single highest-value change you can make.
