The only fixed cost

Google's developer registration fee is a one-time payment made directly to Google. It is not something a testing service can discount, bundle, or waive, so treat any offer that includes it as a line item rather than a favour.

The part that varies

Testing itself is priced on effort, not on software. Four things move the number:

  • Tester count. Twelve is the floor. If you want spares so a drop-out never touches your count, that is more people.
  • Window length. Fourteen continuous days is the requirement. Anything shorter does not satisfy it, so there is nothing to save here.
  • Replacement cover. Holding the count means monitoring it daily and swapping anyone who goes quiet. That is ongoing work across the whole window.
  • Reporting depth. Opt-in timestamps and daily activity take more effort to produce than a headcount.

Why cheap prices are a false economy

Twelve people opening an app on fourteen separate days is roughly 168 genuine sessions. If a quote is far below the cost of paying real people for that work, the difference is being made up somewhere: emulated devices, brand-new accounts, or installs that never turn into activity. Any of those can cost you the whole window, and the registration fee with it.

How to compare two quotes properly

Ignore the headline price until both quotes answer the same five questions.

  1. How many testers will be opted in at the lowest point of the window?
  2. What happens if a tester goes inactive on day eight?
  3. What exactly do I receive at the end, in writing?
  4. Do you ever need my account credentials or signing key?
  5. What is your position on refunds if the window breaks through no fault of mine?

A quote that cannot answer those is not cheaper. It is just less specified, and the missing parts land on your release date.

Doing it for nothing

The cheapest option is real: recruit twelve people yourself, keep them opted in, and run the full window at no cost beyond your time. It works. The failure mode is not the setup, it is day nine, when two people have quietly uninstalled and your count is sitting at ten.

If your network is strong enough to survive that, you do not need to pay anyone. If it is not, that is precisely what the paid option is for.
