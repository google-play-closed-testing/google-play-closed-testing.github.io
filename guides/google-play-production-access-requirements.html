Production access requirements

Production access is a review, not a milestone you unlock. Here is what the application actually looks at, and how to answer it with the evidence your test produced.

Get started Full walkthrough

The gating requirement

For personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023, the production access option stays locked until a closed test has run with at least 12 testers opted in for 14 continuous days. Confirm the exact wording on your own console screen, because the rule is defined by Google and can change.

What the application asks about

The questions are not a quiz. They are there to establish that a real test happened. In practice you are being asked to account for three things:

  • The app and its audience. What it does and who it is for.
  • The test itself. How many testers you had, and how the window ran.
  • What you learned. What testers reported and what you changed as a result.

Specific beats polished

Compare "tested with 12 users and improved the app" against "12 testers opted in on 3 March; two reported a crash on launch with a Xiaomi device; fixed in build 14; no further crashes in the remaining nine days". The second one describes a test. The first one describes a wish.

Build the answers while the window runs

This is the part people get wrong. The material you need is produced during the fortnight, not after it. Keep a running note with:

  1. The date each tester opted in, so you can state the window precisely.
  2. Evidence of activity across the days, not just the install date.
  3. The device models and Android versions involved.
  4. Each piece of feedback, and the change you made in response.

Common reasons an application stalls

  • The test window was not continuousThe opted-in count dropped below twelve at some point, so the qualifying period restarted.
  • The testers were not opted inPeople were added to the list but never joined through the opt-in link, so they never counted.
  • The test did not run long enoughFourteen continuous days is the requirement. A shorter window does not qualify, regardless of tester count.
  • The answers describe no actual testingGeneric responses that could have been written without a test at all.
  • Store listing problemsIncomplete listing information, policy issues in the app itself, or assets that do not match what the app does.

If the answer is no

Read the stated reason before changing anything. A rejection for testing reasons and a rejection for app policy reasons need opposite responses. If the cause was the test, run a full continuous window again with testers who are genuinely active, and reapply with the record in hand. Adding names to a finished window does not repair it.

No one can guarantee this

Approval depends on your app, your listing, and your answers as well as your test. Any service promising a guaranteed outcome is promising something it does not control.
