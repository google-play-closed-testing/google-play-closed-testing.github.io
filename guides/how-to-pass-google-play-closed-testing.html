How to pass Google Play closed testing

The whole thing end to end, from an empty console to a submitted production access request, without the parts that quietly reset your clock.

Get started Adding testers

Before you start

Two minutes of checking here saves you a fortnight of the wrong work.

  • Confirm the requirement applies to your account type, and read the wording on your own console screen.
  • Have more than twelve testers lined up, not exactly twelve.
  • Know which Google account each tester will use, because that is the account that has to open the opt-in link.
  • Make sure your bundle uploads cleanly to a testing track before you invite anybody.

The walkthrough

  1. Open the testing section. In Play Console, select your app, then Testing, then Closed testing.
  2. Create the track. Add a new closed track and upload your app bundle to it.
  3. Add your tester list. Either individual Google addresses or a Google Group. Add spares.
  4. Publish the track. Nothing is visible to testers until you publish. This is the step people skip and then wonder why the link does nothing.
  5. Copy the opt-in link. It appears on the track page once published. This is the link testers open, signed in to the account you listed.
  6. Get twelve opted in. Opted in means joined through the link and installed from the Play Store listing. Watch the count move.
  7. Hold it for fourteen continuous days. Check daily. Anyone who leaves has to be replaced without the count dropping below twelve.
  8. Apply for production access. Once the twelve have held for the full window, submit the request and answer the questions.

The three details that decide everything

Opted in, not listed. Continuous, not cumulative. Twelve at the lowest point, not twelve at the start. Almost every failed window breaks one of those three and nothing else.

Day by day

Day 0. Setup and first opt-ins. This is the only day with real admin in it.

Days 1 to 13. Maintenance. Check the opted-in count, confirm people are still active, replace anyone who has gone quiet. It takes about a minute if the testers are reliable.

Day 14 and after. With twelve testers held continuously, the production access step becomes available. Apply while you still have the testers opted in.

The five ways this breaks

  • A tester used the wrong Google account. The app never appears for them, so their opt-in never registers.
  • Someone installed from a file. An install that did not come from the Play listing does not count.
  • The count dipped. Two people left on day nine while you were not watching, and the window restarted.
  • The track was never published. The opt-in link leads nowhere and nothing begins.
  • Internal testing was used instead. It is a different track and it does not satisfy the requirement.

What to have ready for the application

By the time you apply you should be able to state, without digging: how many testers were opted in, across what dates, what they reported, and what you changed as a result. If you can say that plainly, the form stops being intimidating.

Keep the evidence

Save the notes, the feedback, and the dates as the window runs, not afterwards. Reconstructing a fortnight of activity from memory is the hardest part of a rejection response.
